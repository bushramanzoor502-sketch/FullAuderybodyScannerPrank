How it works

From body part to x-ray layer in five taps

  1. Pick a body type and portion

    Choose Male, Female or Other, then the upper body (head to abdomen), lower body (pelvis to feet) or the full body.

  2. Choose the view and body part

    Front or back, then tap a label on the reference diagram: chest, spine, knee, hand and many more.

  3. Select an anatomy system

    Skeletal, muscular, nervous, circulatory, digestive or respiratory, and start the scan.

  4. Explore the result

    Slide between the photo and the x-ray layer, switch systems, read the key structures, then save or share it. It is kept in My Scans for later.

Photo X-Ray

Drag the slider, or focus it and use the arrow keys.

Generate X-Ray with AI

Your photo, seen through a new layer

Pick an anatomy style, then choose a photo from your gallery or take one with your camera, and FullAudery maps that system onto it.

  1. Choose a template

    Any of the six systems: skeleton, muscles, nerves, vessels, digestion or lungs.

  2. Add your photo

    Pick from your photos, or take a new one. A full body shot, facing the camera in good light, gives the best result.

  3. Compare, save or share

    The result opens in the same compare viewer, marked as AI generated. Results can vary each time, even for the same photo.

For entertainment and learning only

FullAudery is not a real x-ray, a body scanner or a medical device. No camera is used during a Quick X-Ray and your body is not actually scanned: every visual is a simulated illustration chosen from your selections. AI x-rays are artistic renderings made from your photo and can be inaccurate, incomplete or anatomically incorrect.

Nothing in the app can diagnose anything or be used for treatment or any health decision. For any medical concern, consult a qualified healthcare professional.

Privacy by design

Your scans stay on your phone

FullAudery has no account, no ads and no analytics SDK. Your scan history, AI x-rays and settings live in the app's private storage. A photo only leaves your device if the cloud AI service is used to render an AI x-ray, or when you choose to share a result.

Local history

Up to 50 scans and 30 AI x-rays, kept in private app storage and removed with the app.

No account needed

No email, no password and no sign in in front of the anatomy.

Minimal permissions

Internet for AI rendering, and storage access only on Android 9 and below to save images.

No camera permission

Photos come from Android's own photo picker or camera app, only when you ask.

Help centre

Support & quick guides

The things people ask about most: getting a good result, saving it, and managing your history.

Run your first scan

On the dashboard tap Quick X-Ray, then follow the five steps: body type, portion, view, body part and anatomy system. Tap Start Scan and the result opens when the animation finishes.

Better AI x-rays

Use a clear, well lit photo of one person facing the camera, with the whole body in frame. If the body cannot be detected, try another photo. You can always Choose another photo from the result.

Save to gallery

On a result tap Save to gallery. Images go to Pictures/FullAudery. On Android 9 and below, allow the storage permission when asked, or the image cannot be saved.

Manage My Scans

Your recent work appears on the dashboard; tap See all for the full list. Open any item to view it again, or delete it. The oldest entries drop off once the list is full.

Report a bad result

Tap the flag at the top of the result screen, choose a reason (inaccurate, low quality, offensive or something else) and add details if you like.

Moving to a new phone

History is stored on your device, so save the results you want to keep to your gallery first. There is no cloud account to sign back into.

Questions

Frequently asked

Does FullAudery really scan my body?

No. A Quick X-Ray does not use the camera and does not scan you. The images are simulated illustrations picked from your body type, view, body part and system choices. It is made for entertainment and curiosity.

Is this a medical device?

No. FullAudery is not a medical device and cannot diagnose anything. Its images are not medical images and must not be used for diagnosis, treatment or any health decision. Please consult a doctor for any health concern.

How does Generate X-Ray with AI work?

You pick an anatomy template and a photo. The app renders an x-ray style layer onto the person in the photo, either with Google's Gemini image model when that service is available, or on your device using pose detection to bend the anatomy artwork to the body. The result is an artistic rendering, not a real x-ray.

Why does my AI x-ray look wrong?

AI output can be inaccurate or incomplete, and results vary each time, even for the same photo. A full body photo facing the camera in good light works best. Use the flag button to report anything misleading or inappropriate.

Does FullAudery need internet?

Quick X-Ray scans, the anatomy layers and My Scans all work offline. An internet connection is used for AI x-ray rendering with the cloud model and for opening links such as the store page.

Where are my scans saved?

In the app's private storage on your phone. Results you choose to save to the gallery go to Pictures/FullAudery.

What happens to my history if I uninstall?

It is deleted with the app. Images you saved to your gallery stay in your gallery.

"Couldn't save the image"

On Android 9 and below, allow the storage permission when asked. On newer Android no permission is needed; check that your phone has free storage and try again.
